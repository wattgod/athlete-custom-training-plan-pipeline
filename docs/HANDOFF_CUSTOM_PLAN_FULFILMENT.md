# Handoff — custom plan fulfilment, and what stands between here and hands-off

**Status: adversarially reviewed by GPT-5.6-sol, NO-GO on the first draft.** Ten
blockers; the confirmed ones are folded in and marked inline. Two were
corrections to my own claims — the gate does more than I said, and I could not
prove a blocker I asserted had fired. Read §3 and §5 with that in mind.

Written 2026-08-06 after fulfilling order `cs_live_a12JPpqG…` (Monika Renk,
$105, purchased 2026-08-04). Delivered and sent.

**The target state**, in the coach's words: *when someone buys a custom plan, I
open their TrainingPeaks account, sanity-check it, then open my email drafts and
find a message to them confirming the plan is built, with details and the guide
attached.*

This document says what happened, what we shipped, and precisely what is missing
between today and that.

## 1. What happened

### The delivered package was not usable

The pipeline generated a full package in ~20s. It could not be sent. Fourteen
defects are filed in `MONIKA_RENK_PIPELINE_FINDINGS.md`; the ones that mattered:

- **A fabricated FTP.** She submitted `ftp: ""` and `powerOrHr: "hr"`. The
  profile came out `ftp_watts: 127, ftp_estimated: true`, and all 34 ZWO files
  plus the staged TP manifest were anchored to it. 2.15 W/kg for a 45-year-old
  riding 7–10 h/week who wants to compete.
- **Fabricated equipment.** `devices: [power, meter, hr, strap]` — a hardcoded
  string, whitespace-split into four tokens. The form never asks about a power
  meter.
- **Her stated schedule inverted.** She listed long-ride days Mon/Tue/Sun and
  interval days Wed/Thu. The plan put intervals on Tue *and* Sun — four interval
  days a week.
- **Structural holes.** No race-day workout at all. Race week contained two
  sessions. Two FTP tests in week 1. Two workouts dated before the order was
  placed. None of the 11 CRITICAL compliance rules caught any of it.
- **Race facts unverified.** `race_provenance_issue` recorded, `verified_at:
  null` — and shipped. Her "75 miles" was paired with the 89-mile course's
  elevation, because the race record carries one course for a three-course event.

### The athlete's own account was a second source of wrongness

TP athlete **2947583**, coached by Matti, had: zero workouts across 2024–2026,
`expireOn 2019-11-18`, **age 19** (she is 45), a **2019 FTP of 197 W**, and HR
zones of threshold 148 / max 200 auto-derived from that wrong age. Nothing in
the pipeline inspects the destination account.

### Then I made it worse

Three times I rebuilt something the repo already had:

|---|---|
| Generic interval helpers | `archetype_registry.py` — 100 archetypes, 600 variations, plus 525 ZWO files |
| A whole guide generator | `training_guide_builder.py` — 16 sections, conditional altitude/heat/masters/women |

I am not putting a percentage on what this cost — an earlier draft did and the

## 3. Two findings that change the plan of record

### The branches were already merged

`plan-truth-fixes` and `plan-ir-v0` were carried in notes as "written, unmerged,
awaiting merge." Both are obsolete, but for different reasons — worth stating
precisely, because "no new files" alone would not prove it:

- **`plan-ir-v0` is a git ancestor of `origin/main`.** Fully contained. Nothing
  to merge, and `git diff origin/main...plan-ir-v0` is empty.
- **`plan-truth-fixes` is NOT an ancestor** — it holds 4 commits absent from
  main's history. But its *content* landed independently, and main is now
  **ahead** of it: `fueling_policy.py` is **204 lines on main vs 196 on the
  branch**, and includes `_duration_ceiling`, the >8 h carb step-down that
  branch's final commit was supposed to deliver. `PLAN_TRUTH_SPEC.md` and
  `PLAN_TRUTH_HANDOFF.md` are both on main.

So merging `plan-truth-fixes` would **regress** main, not advance it. A trial
merge produced three conflicts against work main had since done independently
(`generate_athlete_package.py` plus two add/add test collisions). Aborted.

`webhook/fulfillment_state.py` — J1 — landed **2026-07-14**, three weeks before
this order. **There is nothing to merge; both branches can be deleted.**

### The gate exists and works — but only over the path nobody used

`intake_to_plan.py:3307` collects blockers and calls `set_generation_blockers`.
It blocks on `RACE_UNMATCHED`, availability review issues, `RACE_STALE`, a brand
conflict, and quality-gate criticals.

**Correction, after adversarial review.** An earlier draft said the gate "did not
stop anything." That is false. `BLOCKED_REVIEW` enforces a real sequence:

- approval requires a waiver covering **every** blocker;
- application requires `APPROVED`;
- `/api/confirm` returns **409 — "Plan must be APPLIED before confirmation"**;
- Phase 1 hard-disables `tools/tp_apply_order.py` job emission and
  `tools/tp_apply_driver.js` execution. The coach applies manually in the
  TrainingPeaks UI, then records APPLIED through the authenticated transition
  with evidence. Automated apply returns only through the Phase 4/5 worker.

The accurate statement is narrower and worse: **the gate governs the tracked
fulfilment path, and nothing forces anyone onto that path.** It does not prevent
package creation, the coach download link, or a manual TrainingPeaks import —
exactly the route this order took.

A second correction: I cannot prove `RACE_STALE` was persisted for her order. Her
original `fulfillment_status.json` no longer exists, and state-write failures are
caught and downgraded to warnings while the pipeline still reports success. The
code path would produce it; that is an inference, not evidence.

Three gaps remain regardless:

1. **Blocked plans are still fully packaged and offered.** The coach notification
   for a blocked order still carries a download link, "Create athlete in
   TrainingPeaks", "Import ZWO files" and a `/api/confirm` curl — and does
   **not** list the blockers or say how to approve or waive them. The email
   routes around the gate.
2. **There is no FTP blocker.** `ftp_estimated` is set at line 839 and read twice
   for email copy. It is never passed to the gate.
3. **`RACE_UNMATCHED` checks the race slug, not the course.** "mammoth-tuff"
   matched at 1.0, so "75 miles matches no course on this race" sailed through.

**The machinery is built. Specific rules were never plugged into it.**

## 4. What stands between here and hands-off

The target has four steps. Here is the honest state of each.

### Step 1 — Plan is built on their TP account · **hardest, and blocked on a decision**

`delivery/trainingpeaks/adapter.py` exists — 140 lines, `apply` / `verify` /
`apply_and_mark_applied`, upsert with readback and an idempotency op-log, tested
against an in-process fake server. That is real and reusable.

**But it authenticates with `Authorization: Bearer {token}` against a
configurable `base_url`, and no such durable credential exists.** Everything I
did today went through `tpapi.trainingpeaks.com` — the web app's internal API,
authenticated by the coach's browser session cookies, plus a short-lived bearer
token scraped from the SPA for calendar-note endpoints. **That token expired
twice during a single working session.**

So there are two roads, and this is a business decision, not an engineering one:

- **TrainingPeaks partner API access.** Durable credentials, supportable,
  requires a commercial conversation with TrainingPeaks.
- **A headless browser worker holding coach credentials.** Works today, but is
  fragile (token refresh, SPA changes), needs secure credential storage, and is
  worth checking against TP's terms before it becomes production infrastructure.

`docs/followups/AUTOMATED_TRAININGPEAKS_FULFILLMENT_TICKET.md` already specifies
what must be defined first — identity/account linking, idempotency, rollback,
audit, manual fallback. That ticket is sound and still unimplemented. **Nothing
downstream of this step can be automated until this is chosen.**

Also unsolved: **athlete identity.** She already had an account under the coach.
A new buyer may have no account, an account under a different email, or a
dormant one with stale thresholds. Nothing today looks.

### Step 2 — Guide generated and published · **nearly there**

Generation is already one command. Publishing is currently manual: worktree, copy
into `docs/guides/<slug>/`, PDF, commit, push, wait for Pages.

Straightforward to script end-to-end. Two things to fix while doing it: the PDF
render is not deterministic (Chrome paginated the same HTML at 46 and 48 pages
depending on whether webfonts loaded), and the `noindex` decision should become
policy rather than a per-guide judgment.

### Step 3 — Gmail draft with the guide attached · **easy, and not yet started**

Nothing in the pipeline touches Gmail. Today's draft was composed through browser
automation, which is not how this should run in production.

The Gmail API creates drafts with attachments directly. `webhook/app.py` already
sends via Resend, so email infrastructure exists — but a *draft for coach review*
is a different thing from a *sent transactional email*, and only the second
exists today.

### Step 4 — Coach sanity-checks · **needs a review surface**

QC'ing this order meant reading a 90 KB guide, a 46-workout calendar and a
coaching brief. That does not survive five orders a day. What is needed is one
page, ranked by what the pipeline was least sure about:

```
MONIKA RENK · Mammoth Tuff · 19 Sep · $105 · 6wk
  ⚠ FTP absent — no power meter. Plan is RPE/HR anchored.   [confirm]
  ⚠ Course "75mi" matched no course on this race.           [confirm]
  ⚠ Race facts unverified — no source URL.                  [confirm]
  ⚠ TP account dormant since 2019; thresholds stale.        [confirm]
  ✓ Schedule honoured: Sat off, Wed/Thu quality, Sun long
  ✓ 46 sessions · 55.8h · peak wk 4 · recovery 57% of load
```

## 5. Ordered work

**1. Make the gate reach the coach, and make blocked mean blocked.** Not small —
an earlier draft called this "three small changes" and that was wrong. It spans:
   - add `FTP_ESTIMATED` as a blocking issue (this part *is* small);
   - make the coach notification **state-aware**: list blocker IDs and messages,
     give approve/waive/apply controls, and never show the confirm command
     before `APPLIED`;
   - decide and implement what "released" means for a blocked plan across
     deliverable persistence, customer ZIP creation, job status,
     `/api/order-status`, `/api/download`, regeneration and stale-ZIP
     invalidation — separately for coach, customer, TP executor and guide
     publisher;
   - course matching needs a `courses[]` model on the race record first; the
     snapshot schema is one record per race today.

**2. Fix the structural holes** the compliance rules missed entirely: no
race-day workout, a two-session race week, two FTP tests in week 1, sessions
dated before delivery. Add each as a rule.

**3. Decide the TrainingPeaks credential question.** Partner API or browser
worker. Everything hands-off depends on it and it is not an engineering call.

**4. Build the one-page review surface.** Ranked by uncertainty.

**5. Script guide publish + Gmail draft.** Both are mechanical once 3 is decided.

**6. Questionnaire v2** — the spec is **NO-GO** until rewritten. sol found it
targets the wrong parser: paid orders go questionnaire JSON → markdown →
`intake_to_plan.py`, never `create_profile_from_form.py`, and that adapter has
nowhere to put a per-day grid. Null FTP is also not end-to-end safe today
(`generate_plan_preview.py` calls `float(None)` and the pipeline swallows it).

**7. Fix archetype *selection*, not archetype *use*.** An earlier draft said to
"point generation at the archetype library." Backward — it already does
(`generate_athlete_package.py` and `workout_mapper.py` both call the registry).
The real gap is which archetypes and levels get chosen: Matti replaced the
generated sessions with harder, more varied ones **from the same library**. The
work is in the selection matrix and level ladder, not the wiring.

## 5b. Missing from the target design entirely

Adversarial review found these absent, and each can break the hands-off flow:

- **Athlete identity is on the critical path, not a footnote.** A buyer may have
  no TP account, one under a different email, a dormant one, or one not coached
  by Matti. Nothing today looks. This gates step 1, not step 4.
- **`noindex` is not privacy.** The guide is world-readable to anyone with the
  URL on a public repo. If athlete guides should be private, they need auth or a
  private host — a meta tag only asks crawlers politely.
- **Refunds, cancellations and plan updates.** What happens to a placed
  calendar on refund? What happens when a plan is regenerated after workouts are
  already on the athlete's calendar? Undefined.
- **Multi-brand.** The webhook supports gravelgod and roadielabs, with unknown
  origins defaulting to gravelgod. The target flow above is written as if there
  is one brand.
- **Ordering conflict.** "Plan auto-built on their TP account, coach
  sanity-checks after" inverts the current model, where approval precedes
  application. Either the model changes deliberately or the target does.
